HIIT Training Guide

Master High-Intensity Interval Training for maximum results

What is HIIT?

High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT) alternates between short bursts of intense exercise and brief recovery periods. This training method maximizes calorie burn in minimal time and continues burning calories even after your workout ends.

The Basic Formula:
WORK + REST = REPEAT

Benefits

  • • Burns more calories in less time
  • • Boosts metabolism for hours after
  • • Improves cardiovascular health
  • • Builds endurance and strength
  • • No equipment required

Best For

  • • Busy schedules (20-30 min workouts)
  • • Fat loss and conditioning
  • • Breaking through plateaus
  • • Improving athletic performance
  • • Home workouts

Popular HIIT Protocols

Tabata

4 min total
20s work 10s rest x 8 rounds

The gold standard of HIIT. Maximum intensity, minimum time. Perfect for finishers.

30/30

10 min total
30s work 30s rest x 10 rounds

Balanced work-to-rest ratio. Great for beginners and intermediate athletes.

EMOM (Every Minute on the Minute)

10+ min
40s work 20s rest x 10+ rounds

Start each minute with a set number of reps. Rest for the remainder. Scalable difficulty.

Little Method

27 min total
60s work 75s rest x 12 rounds

Research-backed protocol for improving VO2 max and overall fitness.

HIIT Tips for Success

Go ALL OUT During Work

HIIT only works if you're giving maximum effort during work intervals. Aim for 80-95% intensity.

Actually Rest During Rest

Don't do active recovery unless specified. Use rest periods to catch your breath and prepare for the next burst.

Limit to 2-3 Sessions Per Week

HIIT is demanding. Your body needs time to recover. More isn't always better.

Warm Up First

Always warm up for 5-10 minutes before HIIT to prevent injury and optimize performance.

Form Over Speed

Maintain proper form even at high intensity. Bad form leads to injuries and reduced effectiveness.
